# Jaxon's Maths Adventure: Approved Visual Direction and Full Graphics Rollout

## 1. Approval and objective

The owner has reviewed the side-by-side visual comparisons between commits `63f787f` and `0444e1b`.

**The revised visual direction at commit `0444e1b` is APPROVED for the wider graphical rollout.**

The latest changes represent a substantial improvement. Continue development using this standard, while aiming to match the 16 previously approved artwork references as closely as possible.

This is approval to continue development, not approval to publish an incomplete game.

The objective is to finish transforming Jaxon's Maths Adventure into a detailed, engaging, age-appropriate voxel-style educational game rather than a collection of maths forms with illustrated backgrounds.

Do not restart the artwork design process or ask for approval of concepts already agreed.

## 2. Approved graphical principles

Apply these rules consistently:

- Detailed, polished voxel and isometric-style environments.
- Original, age-appropriate artwork suitable for learners approximately 8–14 years old.
- Characters with teenage proportions rather than preschool proportions.
- Consistent perspective, lighting, scale and grounding.
- People populating the environments, with meaningful roles.
- Interactions integrated into illustrated scenes wherever practical.
- Objects that visibly respond to being selected, moved, opened, placed or connected.
- Minimal separation between the game world and its educational activities.
- Consistent visual quality across environments, menus and interactive tools.

Do not revert to basic CSS/SVG shapes as a substitute for the approved illustrated style.

Simple SVG or CSS remains appropriate for precise mathematical diagrams, responsive interface controls and accessibility features.

## 3. Complete the connected world map

Implement the previously approved navigation design.

**Base Camp must sit at the centre of a large connected world map**, with visible bridges, pathways or portals leading to all nine maths worlds.

The worlds are:

1. Number Forest
2. Fraction Desert
3. Algebra Peaks
4. Geometry Kingdom
5. Ratio Island
6. Statistics City
7. Probability Portal
8. Measurement Mines
9. Practical Maths Town

Requirements:

- All nine worlds must remain visible, whether locked or unlocked.
- Each world must have its own recognisable environment.

- Locked worlds should clearly display their requirements.
- World progress should be visually represented.
- Completing missions should visibly restore damaged areas.
- Learners must be able to return to Base Camp without losing progress.

Do not use the alternative world names that appeared in some early generated concept artwork.

## 6. Complete all nine world environments

Extend the approved visual quality to each maths world.

Each world needs:

- A distinctive detailed environment.
- Recognisable landmarks and locations.
- Characters appropriate to the setting.
- Maths objects that belong naturally in that world.
- Clear mission entrances and progression.
- Functional interactive activities.
- Visual improvements as missions are completed.

Specific examples:

### Number Forest
Build number bridges, place-value structures, treehouses, grouped objects and forest-based puzzles.

### Fraction Desert
Use fraction-based water channels, supply systems, shaded objects and geometric constructions.

### Algebra Peaks
Use the approved mature industrial freight railway style, mountain infrastructure, mechanical puzzles and manipulable equation components.

Do not revert to a childish toy-train appearance.

### Geometry Kingdom
Use castles, buildings, shapes, rotations, construction puzzles and coordinate-based activities.

### Ratio Island
Use island resources, supply groups, transport and proportional reasoning activities.

### Statistics City
Use buildings, data displays, charts and interactive information systems.

### Probability Portal
Use mysterious portals, spinners, outcomes and visually represented probability experiments.

### Measurement Mines
Use mining equipment, containers, measuring tools, lengths, masses, volumes and related machinery.

### Practical Maths Town
Retain the approved connected Town, including populated locations, recognisable buildings, interactive interiors and meaningful everyday maths.

- Scary and mysterious rather than funny.
- The same villain damages all nine worlds.
